# The Impact of Gasoline Prices on Gasoline Demand in the United States (1993–2026)

**Author:** Roya Rezaie

## Executive Summary & Findings
This research investigates the short-run price elasticity of U.S. gasoline demand using monthly observations from April 1993 through July 2026 from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) and FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis).

* **Key Finding:** The preferred econometric model yields a price elasticity of **-0.066** ($p = 0.001$), indicating that gasoline demand is highly price-inelastic in the short run.
* **Managerial Guidance:** A 10% increase in pump price decreases quantity demanded by less than 1%. Retail fuel volume is governed primarily by seasonality and macroeconomic activity rather than price adjustments; retailers should protect fuel margins and avoid ruinous price discounting.
